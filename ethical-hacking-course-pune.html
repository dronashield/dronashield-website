Course Overview

What This Ethical Hacking Course Covers

DronaShield's Ethical Hacking course is our flagship foundation-to-advanced program for anyone who wants to genuinely understand how attackers break into networks, applications and devices, and how defenders stop them. Delivered from our Baner campus in Pune, this ethical hacking course is built for students who may have zero prior security background as much as for IT professionals looking for a structured, lab-heavy path into the field, unlike narrower certification-prep tracks that assume you already know the basics.

The course opens with solid networking and Linux foundations, because every attack technique taught later, from scanning to exploitation, only makes sense once you understand how packets, ports, services and permissions actually work. From there, students progress through reconnaissance, vulnerability analysis, system hacking, malware behaviour, web application attacks, wireless security and an introduction to cloud attack surfaces, always paired with a hands-on lab so concepts are practiced immediately rather than left as theory.

Batches run in a hybrid format so working professionals and students across Baner, Balewadi, Wakad, Hinjewadi, Aundh and Pashan can attend evening or weekend sessions without disrupting their day job or degree. Every learner gets access to an isolated hacking range stocked with intentionally vulnerable machines, allowing safe, legal practice against realistic scenarios using tools like Nmap, Metasploit, Burp Suite, Wireshark and Kali Linux.

Graduates leave with a portfolio of completed lab exercises, a DronaShield Institute completion certificate, and a strong enough foundation to move directly into our CEH v13 training or specialised VAPT and SOC analyst tracks. Our placement cell also supports job-ready students with resume reviews, mock interviews and introductions to hiring partners across Pune's growing cyber security industry.

Before You Start

Prerequisites

This Ethical Hacking course is deliberately built to accept genuine beginners, so the bar to entry is low, but a few things will help you get more out of it.

  • Basic familiarity with using a computer and browsing the internet.
  • Willingness to learn command-line basics; the first modules teach Linux and networking from scratch.
  • A laptop capable of running a virtual machine, 8GB RAM or more recommended for lab work.
  • No prior certification, degree or programming background is required to enroll.
